Microsoft 365

From the server closet to the cloud

I've supported Microsoft servers for decades: Windows Server, Exchange, shared file servers, the works. Today most small offices don't need a server at all. Microsoft 365 does the same jobs with less hardware, fewer headaches and access from anywhere.

File server→SharePoint · Exchange server→Exchange Online · My Documents→OneDrive

SharePoint & OneDrive

Your shared company files move off the old server into SharePoint, with the folders and permissions people are used to. Each person's own files live in OneDrive and sync to every device they use.

Email, Teams & calendars

Exchange Online replaces the in-house mail server. Shared mailboxes, calendars and Teams are set up the way your office actually works.

A careful move

Because I know the servers you're leaving, I can plan the move properly: what to bring, what to clean up, and how to switch over without losing a day of work.

Backup & 24/7 monitoring

Microsoft 365 backup through CloudAlly, and around-the-clock monitoring of your Microsoft 365 accounts for logins from unexpected places, backed by Kaseya's 24/7 monitoring center. If something looks wrong, I get the alert.

Why it matters

When an employee at one of my law firm clients had their Microsoft 365 account taken over from Germany, the monitoring service alerted me right away. I locked the account down before any firm or client data was taken.

About

Tom Shubert

A computer nerd since age 13, I've been doing IT since 1982, through every change from mainframes and in-house servers to today's cloud.

  1. 1982After graduating from Emory University, began consulting as a contract programmer on mainframe and minicomputer projects for Atlanta companies, including special projects at The Coca-Cola Company's headquarters.
  2. 1984Co-founded a microcomputer consulting company that built PCs and set up and supported office networks for companies across the United States.
  3. Mid-'80sAs a Novell Direct Gold Reseller, installed some of the first office networks, replacing big computers and dumb terminals.
  4. 1988Moved from Atlanta to Los Angeles and went in-house, running the network and computer systems for a securities firm.
  5. 1994Went back to independent IT consulting for small businesses, and started YourWEB Services for websites.
  6. TodayAbout 100 businesses and individuals supported in recent years, many moving off their servers and onto Microsoft 365.

I work with small businesses of up to about 50 people. They range from one-person mortgage and insurance offices to law firms of 20 attorneys and staff, and even a high-tech defense contractor division with a parent company overseas. I'm a jack of all trades by design: one person who understands the network, the phones, the email and the website, tells you straight what's wrong, and doesn't stop until it's fixed.
